Best Time to Visit Hallstatt: a Month-by-Month Verdict

Most advice about Hallstatt is about the hour you arrive. That advice is correct, and it is on our complete guide to the village. But the hour is a tactic. The month is the decision — because the month decides what is actually open when you get there, and no amount of arriving early fixes a cable car that is switched off.

The short answer: the second half of May into mid-June, and the second half of September into October. Both give you long days, the mountain running, and a village that empties in the evening. If those are impossible, the rest of this page is about what each month costs you.

The village itself does not change with the month. What changes is how much of the mountain above it you can reach — and in the months when that answer is "not much", the village has to carry the visit on its own. It can: the deer-antler pick that came out of the mine and dates to around 5000 BC, the fire of 1750 that took some thirty-five houses and left the Trinity column on the square untouched, the graveyard above the lane that gave an entire European age its name. Our seventeen-stop audio walk runs from the ferry pier to the World Heritage viewpoint in about ninety minutes and is available all year, so you set the pace around the weather and the state of the paths.

The year in one table

The crowd column needs a word of explanation, and it is the honest kind. Hallstatt does not count its visitors. The one official series that exists by month is overnight stays — nights booked in the village — which Statistik Austria publishes per municipality. Those are not day visitors, and day visitors are the ones who fill the lane. So read the column as the seasonality of beds, which is a real and published thing, rather than as a headcount on the street. It is the only month-by-month series anyone publishes, and the shape it describes matches everything else we could verify. The index is Statistik Austria's own, measured against its published 2024 monthly average of 11,621 nights.

Two things jump out. In August the village books about four and a half times as many nights as in November — that is the seasonality of accommodation, not a measurement of how many people are standing in the lane. And December, the month everyone imagines as the quiet one, books more nights than any month from January to April.

How crowded it really gets

The figure you will see everywhere is 1.2 million day visitors a year. It is worth knowing where that number comes from, because it is a calculation rather than a count. The World Heritage management plan converts entries to the village car parks into people, at 2.8 persons per car and 30 per coach. The mayor projected about 1.2 million on that basis for 2025. Anyone arriving by train and ferry is not in it at all.

For a sense of a single bad day, the 2018 transport study in the same document put the maximum calculated daily figure at around 9,000 people. But the plan's own arithmetic says it better than any round number: between the ferry pier at Lahn and the northern photo point live 237 people, and on an average day each of them gets almost 15 visitors. On a peak day, almost 40.

The useful part is what has changed. Coaches have been capped at 54 a day since 2020, booked into slots with a minimum stay of two hours twenty, and in 2024 the municipality's own transport planner said daily coach numbers had halved since 2018. Cars and trains have no quota at all. So the crowd you meet in 2027 is less a wall of coach groups arriving at eleven and more a steady all-day flow of cars — which is exactly the kind of crowd that thins at the edges of the day rather than in the middle of the week.

And the season itself has stretched. The management plan is blunt about it: since 2018 mass day tourism in Hallstatt has become, in its words, an almost year-round phenomenon. Choosing a quiet month no longer means choosing an empty village. It means choosing a village where the quiet hours are genuinely quiet.

The two windows worth planning around

Mid-May to mid-June. The mountain comes back in stages rather than all at once, and the overnight index is still only a fifth above average while the days are the longest of the year. In mid-June the sun is up for sixteen hours and sets after nine in the evening. That is the best combination the calendar offers: the mountain open, a long evening, and the July wave still a few weeks away.

So the first days of May give you the ice cave and the viewing platform but not the Mammoth Cave and not the top section of the cable car. Everything is running from 4 June, and everything in that table stops again on 1 November.

Mid-September to the end of October. The second window trades daylight for quiet, and the trade is sharper than it looks. By 15 September you are down to twelve and a half hours of light and the index has fallen from 182 to 121; in October it is 95 — the only month of the year that sits below the annual average while the mountain is still open. That is the whole case for October, and it is worth stating plainly, because every quieter month on the list has the cable cars switched off.

The costs are real. October has four hours less daylight than mid-June, and the ferry, the mine and the funicular all shorten their day from the beginning of the month, so the same plan has to fit a smaller box. Against that: the larches turn, the lake goes still, and the village is carrying about a fifth fewer booked beds than in September. We would take the first half of the month over the second, for the extra light and the better odds on weather.

Both windows share the thing that actually matters: they are shoulder months with the mountain open. That combination exists for about ten weeks of the year in total, and the rest of this page is a series of trade-offs.

The six weeks nobody warns you about

This is the one piece of timing that catches people out, and it is invisible unless you build the calendar yourself. Each operator publishes its own season dates clearly enough. Put them side by side and a gap of nearly six weeks appears, in which most of the mountain is unavailable.

On 1 November the entire Dachstein side switches off at once: all three cable-car sections, the Giant Ice Cave, the Mammoth Cave, the 5fingers platform and the Gosaukammbahn. Krippenstein does not reopen until 19 December. The Dachstein West ski area opens on 4 December. So between 2 November and 18 December there is a six-week gap in which the big day out across the lake does not exist, and the operator publishes nothing about that gap at all — the dates just stop and start again.

The salt mine behaves differently, and this is where our own earlier reading was wrong. Salzwelten does not close in early November. On the operator's own Hallstatt page the mine runs from 29 August to 1 November with the last guided tour starting at 16:30, and then from 2 November to 6 January with the last tour at 15:00, closed on 24 and 31 December. The mine and the funicular are published as two separate tables, and the funicular's last descent is 18:00 and then 16:30 over the same two periods; the last ascent for a tour is half an hour before the tour starts. There is no maintenance or closure notice on that page, and the Skywalk has no hours or price of its own — it is reached by the funicular.

So in November the mountain keeps its one big attraction, it is a good one, and it is indifferent to weather. The village's own paid sights stay open too: the World Heritage Museum daily at €12, the Bone House at €2 on winter hours. November is thin, not empty.

Which makes November the sharpest trade in the year. It books the fewest beds of any month by a wide margin — an index of 40 against August's 182 — and it is also the month in which the least is available. If you want Hallstatt without the crowd and you do not care about the mountain, November is the answer. If you were planning on ice caves, it is the one month to avoid outright.

The edge of the published year

Salzwelten's table ends on 6 January 2027, and after that date the operator publishes nothing — not an opening date, not a closure. We are writing this in October 2026 and there is no 2027 spring date anywhere. The same is true of the lake boats, which the operator describes as a May-to-September service without giving dates, and of the ferry timetable, which expires on 13 December 2026 when the Austrian rail timetable changes.

We would rather say that plainly than guess. If you are planning for January to April 2027, the museum is the one large indoor thing with published daily hours — 11:00 to 16:00 from January to March, last entry an hour before closing — and everything on the salt mountain needs confirming with the operator for your date.

Winter: the evening window closes before you get there

The standard advice for Hallstatt is to be in the village before half past nine or after half past four, when the day visitors have gone. In summer that second window is a gift. In winter it is not a window at all.

Calculated for the village's own coordinates, the sun sets at 16:14 in mid-December and 16:41 in mid-January, and Hallstatt sits on the west shore under a steep wall, so the lane loses direct sun earlier still. Diffuse daylight holds for a while after that, and then the blue hour, which is its own kind of good — this is not an argument that winter evenings are worthless. It is an argument that the long bright evening window summer gives you does not exist in December. In mid-June the sun sets at 21:05, and the two hours after the last coach pulls out are the best light of the day; in December those two hours are simply night.

So the shape of the advice has to change with the season. From roughly April to October, plan for the evening: arrive in the afternoon, let the village empty around you, stay for the light. From November to February, plan for the morning instead — be out at first light, which in December is not an act of heroism because the sun does not come up until 07:47.

Month by month, in short

January to March. The quietest stretch of the year, index 50 to 60. The mine shuts after 6 January with nothing published beyond. The museum is open daily. The ferry runs — it is, as both the operator and the tourist board put it, the only scheduled boat service kept running through winter on an Austrian lake, on the same timetable it uses in August. Skiing at Dachstein West and Krippenstein until late March. February carries a wrinkle worth knowing: the mayor has said Asian holiday travel, including Lunar New Year, brings a noticeable wave in that month.

April. Index 72, days already nearly fourteen hours long, and the mountain still shut until 1 May. The thaw at this altitude is brown rather than green. Cheap and quiet, and the month we would recommend least of the warm half.

May and June. The first window, opening in stages: the first two cable-car sections, the 5fingers platform and the Giant Ice Cave on 1 May, the Mammoth Cave and the Gosaukammbahn on 9 May, the third cable-car section on 4 June. From early July the Dachstein lifts extend to seven in the evening, but you do not need that in June because the daylight does the work for you.

July and August. Peak, with August the busiest month in the series. The lifts run longest and the lake is warm enough to swim in. Everything else about these two months is a cost. If the school calendar makes the decision for you, the compensation is that these are the months when the evening window is widest — the light holds until nine.

September. Index drops to 121 and keeps falling through the month. The lake boats finish — the operator says May to September, the tourist board says May to October, and nobody publishes a date, so confirm it if a cruise is part of your plan. Everything else runs.

October. Below the yearly average at 95, the mountain open until the 1st of November, and the autumn colour that makes the classic view look like the photographs. The ferry, the mine and the funicular all shorten their day from the beginning of the month, so build the day earlier than you would in June.

November. An overnight index of 40, the low point of the year, and the thinnest list of things to do. See above.

December. Two different months in one. Up to the 18th you are in the gap — no Dachstein, mine on a short day, skiing only from the 4th at Dachstein West. From the 19th Krippenstein opens and the village fills: the index for December as a whole is 89, and the mayor has described Christmas and New Year as being like high summer. The Christmas market itself is one day only, 8 December, and we have a separate guide to that day. The mine closes on the 24th and the 31st.

The hour, inside the month

Once the month is settled, the hour is simple and we will not pad it out: in high season the main lane is shoulder to shoulder between about ten and three, and thins at both ends. Coaches may enter only between 08:00 and 17:00, at a maximum of twelve movements an hour, and must stay at least two hours twenty — so the first groups cannot leave before about 10:20. That is the rhythm you are working around.

What no official source publishes, anywhere, is a busiest day of the week or an hourly profile. We looked. The municipality does not release it and the tourist board does not either, so anyone who tells you Tuesday is quieter than Thursday is guessing. For the part that is documented — which sights change most between the quiet hours and the busy ones — see what to see in Hallstatt.

One night changes which month you need

Here is the part that makes the whole calendar softer. Every trade-off above is a day-tripper's trade-off. Stay the night and the month matters less, because you get the two quiet windows of every day regardless of season.

The numbers say how few people do this. Against that calculated million-plus day visitors, 2024 brought 138,835 overnight stays in the village — nights, not people. The regional tourism report counts 91,825 arrivals across the 2024/25 tourism year at an average stay of a night and a half; an arrival is a check-in at an accommodation, so that is not 91,825 separate individuals either. Hallstatt has around 888 guest beds. However you read the figures, the number of people who sleep here is a small fraction of the number who pass through.

In practice a room in the village buys you the two calmest parts of the day in whichever month you come — early morning and late evening, before the day visitors arrive and after they have gone. It also means booking early, because the village beds are scarce and summer sells out months ahead; Obertraun across the water is the overflow, and it has more than twice Hallstatt's bed capacity.

The other lever is how you arrive. A car gives you the most freedom over your own timing and makes it easy to combine Hallstatt with the rest of the Salzkammergut, with two caveats: the village is closed to visitor traffic, so you park in the numbered lots on the approach, and those lots fill early in high season. It is not the only way to control the clock — coming the evening before, or basing yourself in Obertraun across the water, does the same job without a car. What does not work is a day built around whichever train is convenient: the first useful one from Vienna puts you on the market square around half past nine at the earliest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best month to visit Hallstatt?
The second half of May to mid-June, or the second half of September through October. Both have the mountain running and an index well below the July and August peak. Of the two, the first half of October is our single pick.

What is the quietest month in Hallstatt?
November, on the only month-by-month series anyone publishes: Statistik Austria's overnight index puts it at 40 against an annual average of 100 and an August peak of 182. That measures booked beds rather than people in the lane — no official source publishes day visitors by month — but it is a wide enough gap to be meaningful. The cost is that the Dachstein cable cars, the ice caves and the 5fingers platform are all shut from 2 November until 19 December.

Is Hallstatt worth visiting in winter?
Yes, with two conditions. Know that the salt mine's published season ends on 6 January 2027 and nothing is published beyond that, and know that the sun sets at about 16:14 in mid-December — so the usual advice to come late in the day gives you a dark village rather than a quiet bright one. In winter, plan for the morning.

How bad are the crowds in Hallstatt?
The village does not count visitors; it converts car-park entries into an estimate. On that basis the municipality calculated about 1.2 million day visitors for 2025, and the 2018 transport study put the busiest calculated day at around 9,000 people. The management plan's own framing is sharper: 237 people live in the core, and on a peak day they get almost 40 visitors each.

When is the Hallstatt Christmas market?
One day only — Tuesday 8 December in 2026, on the Marktplatz, 10:00 to 18:00 on the regional tourist board's listing and 18:30 on another of its pages. It is not an Advent season. There is a separate guide to that day on this site.

Does the Hallstatt ferry run all year?
Yes, on one timetable that does not change with the season. The important detail is the direction: the last sailing from the village to the station is 17:45, while 18:30 is the last sailing from the station into the village. The current timetable runs to 13 December 2026.

Is it cheaper to visit Hallstatt off-season?
Rooms are, considerably. Tickets are not — the salt mine and the funicular are the same price in November as in July, and in winter you are paying the same €29 for a funicular to a platform that may be in cloud. The off-season saving is on beds, not on the mountain.

The verdict

If you can choose, choose the first half of October, with mid-May to mid-June a close second. If you cannot choose, the month matters much less than one decision inside it: whether you sleep here. A night in the village turns any month into a good one, and no amount of clever timing turns a four-hour day trip in August into a quiet one.

And if the month is fixed and it happens to be November, go anyway — but go for the village and the mine, not for the mountain, and know before you book that the cable cars across the lake will not be running.

Dates, prices, opening seasons and the crowd figures on this page were checked in October 2026 against the operators' own sites, Statistik Austria, the Upper Austria tourism report and the UNESCO World Heritage management plan. Seasons here change more than in most places — confirm anything your day depends on for your own date.
